## Fertility Guide in Lahore: Causes, Tests and Treatment Options for Couples

Trying to conceive can raise many questions. Are irregular periods affecting ovulation? Can a man have fertility problems without symptoms? Which fertility tests are necessary, and when should a couple consider treatments such as IUI, IVF or ICSI?

Fertility depends on several interconnected factors, including **ovulation, sperm health, the fallopian tubes, the uterus and age**. Some couples identify a clear cause of delayed conception, while others need further investigation before their options become clear.

For couples seeking fertility care in Lahore, the first step is understanding the possible reasons for difficulty conceiving. A structured fertility assessment can help identify relevant factors, avoid unnecessary investigations and guide decisions about appropriate treatment.

## Understanding Fertility and Infertility

Fertility is the ability to achieve a pregnancy. The World Health Organization (WHO) defines infertility as a disease of the male or female reproductive system characterised by failure to achieve pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular, unprotected sexual intercourse.

Infertility can affect someone who has never achieved a pregnancy or someone who has conceived previously but is experiencing difficulty conceiving again.

According to the **[WHO infertility fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/infertility)**, approximately one in six people of reproductive age worldwide experience infertility during their lifetime.

Infertility may involve:

* Female reproductive factors, such as problems with ovulation or the fallopian tubes.
* Male reproductive factors, including problems with sperm production or function.
* Factors affecting both partners.
* Unexplained infertility, when an appropriate evaluation does not identify a clear cause.

A diagnosis of infertility does not automatically mean that pregnancy is impossible. The outlook and treatment options depend on the underlying circumstances, age, investigation results and other clinical factors.

### How Does Conception Occur?

Understanding the process helps explain why fertility assessments examine several aspects of reproductive health.

* **Ovulation:** An ovary releases an egg during the menstrual cycle. Irregular or absent ovulation can reduce the opportunities for conception.
* **Sperm production:** Sperm must be produced and transported through the male reproductive system. Sperm concentration, movement and other characteristics can affect fertility.
* **Fertilisation:** Sperm and egg usually meet in a fallopian tube, where fertilisation can occur.
* **Embryo development and implantation:** Following fertilisation, an embryo develops and may implant in the lining of the uterus.

Problems affecting any of these stages can contribute to difficulty conceiving. However, no single symptom or test necessarily explains the complete picture.

## When to Seek Fertility Advice

The appropriate time to seek an infertility assessment depends on age, medical history and how long a couple has been trying to conceive.

As a general guideline:

* **Under 35:** Seek an assessment after 12 months of regular, unprotected intercourse without conception.
* **Age 35 or older:** Seek an assessment after six months of trying.
* **Known fertility concerns:** Consider earlier assessment if there are irregular or absent periods, a known reproductive condition, previous pelvic surgery, suspected endometriosis or concerns about sperm health.

People over 40 may benefit from more immediate assessment rather than waiting for the usual timeframe.

These timeframes are general guidance, not a reason to delay care when a known medical concern exists.

A fertility consultation does not automatically mean that treatment is necessary. It helps establish which questions need answering and whether further investigation is appropriate.

## Causes of Female Infertility

Female infertility can involve ovulation, ovarian reserve, the fallopian tubes, the uterus or other reproductive and hormonal factors. Several factors may coexist, so an individual assessment is important.

### Ovulation Problems and PCOS

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. When ovulation is irregular or absent, conception may become more difficult.

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that can interfere with ovulation. Some women with PCOS have irregular periods, acne or excess facial hair, while others may have fewer obvious symptoms.

PCOS does not mean that pregnancy is impossible. The appropriate approach depends on whether ovulation is occurring and whether other fertility factors are present.

### Regular Periods but No Pregnancy

Regular menstrual cycles can suggest that ovulation is occurring, but they do not confirm that every aspect of fertility is normal.

Difficulty conceiving may involve sperm health, the fallopian tubes, endometriosis, the uterus, age or other factors.

### Fallopian Tube Problems

The fallopian tubes provide the usual location where sperm and egg meet. Blockage or damage can interfere with this process.

Previous pelvic infections, certain sexually transmitted infections, endometriosis and pelvic surgery may increase the risk of tubal problems.

Depending on the medical history, a clinician may recommend a test to assess whether the tubes are open.

### Uterine Conditions and Endometriosis

Some uterine conditions, including particular fibroids, polyps or structural abnormalities, may affect fertility. Endometriosis can also contribute to difficulty conceiving.

Finding one of these conditions does not automatically mean that it is responsible for infertility or that surgery is necessary. Its significance depends on the individual clinical picture.

### Age and Ovarian Reserve

Female fertility generally declines with age. Changes in egg quantity and quality can influence the likelihood of natural conception and the outcomes of fertility treatment.

Tests such as anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) and antral follicle count may help estimate ovarian reserve and guide treatment planning. They cannot independently predict whether natural conception will occur.

## Causes of Male Infertility

Male fertility is an essential part of the evaluation. Fertility problems should not automatically be attributed to the female partner, because male factors may contribute independently or alongside female factors.

Possible causes include:

* Low sperm concentration or absence of sperm in semen.
* Reduced sperm movement or abnormal sperm morphology.
* Problems affecting sperm production or transport.
* Certain hormonal conditions.
* Previous infections, injuries or reproductive surgery.
* Erectile or ejaculation difficulties that interfere with conception.
* Some medicines and other health-related factors.

The American Urological Association and American Society for Reproductive Medicine guideline recommends evaluating both partners and using the man's reproductive history and semen analysis to guide further assessment.

### Understanding Semen Analysis

Semen analysis commonly evaluates sperm concentration, movement and morphology, alongside other characteristics of the sample.

An abnormal result does not automatically mean that natural conception is impossible. Semen parameters can vary, and repeat testing or further assessment may be appropriate depending on the findings.

## Fertility Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the underlying cause, age, reproductive history, test results and individual preferences. Not every couple needs assisted reproductive treatment.

### 1. Expectant Management and Timing Guidance

Some couples may benefit from advice about the fertile window and timing intercourse. When circumstances are favourable and no condition requiring treatment has been identified, a clinician may discuss continuing to try naturally for an appropriate period.

This approach may not be suitable when age or a known fertility problem makes timely investigation important.

### 2. Ovulation Induction

When ovulation is irregular or absent, selected medicines may help stimulate ovulation. Treatment and monitoring depend on the diagnosis and the response to medication.

Women with PCOS-related anovulatory infertility may benefit from ovulation induction, but other factors, including sperm health and fallopian tube function, must also be considered.

Explore **[Ovulation Induction and PCOS Treatment](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/fertility-treatment/ovulation-induction)**.

Medication should only be used under appropriate clinical supervision.

### 3. Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)

IUI involves placing prepared sperm directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation.

It may be considered in selected circumstances, depending on the cause of infertility, sperm parameters and other clinical findings. It is not suitable for every couple.

### 4. In Vitro Fertilisation (IVF)

IVF involves stimulating the ovaries, collecting eggs, fertilising them in a laboratory and transferring an embryo into the uterus.

It may be considered for various fertility-related conditions, including certain tubal problems, selected cases of male-factor infertility and some cases where other treatments have not been successful.

**IVF is not automatically the best first treatment.** The decision depends on the diagnosis, age, previous treatment and individual circumstances.

ICSI is a laboratory technique used as part of an IVF cycle. An embryologist injects a single sperm directly into an egg.

It may be considered in selected cases involving significant male-factor infertility or previous fertilisation difficulties. It is not automatically necessary for every IVF cycle.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a couple conceive naturally after a year of trying?

Yes, natural conception may still occur. However, an infertility assessment is generally recommended after 12 months of trying if the female partner is under 35, or after six months at age 35 or older. Known fertility concerns may justify earlier evaluation.

### Can regular periods rule out infertility?

No. Regular periods can suggest that ovulation is occurring, but they do not confirm that sperm health, fallopian tube function and other reproductive factors are normal. See our guide to **[regular periods without pregnancy](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/regular-periods-but-not-getting-pregnant)**.

### Does infertility always require IVF?

No. Depending on the cause, options may include monitoring, treatment of an underlying condition, ovulation induction or IUI. IVF may be appropriate in other circumstances. The choice should be based on clinical findings.

### Should both partners undergo fertility testing?

Both partners should generally be evaluated as part of the initial infertility assessment. The specific tests depend on age, history, symptoms and previous findings.

### Does low AMH mean pregnancy is impossible?

No. AMH can help assess ovarian reserve and guide fertility treatment planning, but it cannot independently determine whether natural conception will occur. It should be interpreted alongside age and other clinical findings.

### Can male infertility be treated?

Some causes are treatable, while others may require different management approaches. The outlook depends on the underlying cause and the results of an appropriate evaluation.

### Does a fertility assessment guarantee pregnancy?

No. An assessment helps identify possible causes and guide decisions, but it cannot guarantee conception or a particular treatment outcome.
