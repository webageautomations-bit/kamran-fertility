## Quick Answer: What Does an Andrologist Do?

An andrologist focuses on male reproductive and sexual health, including male infertility, sperm problems, hormonal concerns, erectile difficulties, and other conditions affecting male reproductive function.

Depending on the problem, evaluation may include a medical history, physical examination, semen analysis, hormone testing, or other investigations. A man does not necessarily need every available test.

For fertility concerns, **semen analysis** is often an important starting investigation because it provides information about sperm concentration, movement, shape, semen volume, and other characteristics. WHO provides standardized laboratory guidance for semen examination.

## What Is an Andrologist?

An andrologist is a doctor who focuses on male reproductive health and sexual medicine.

The field of andrology overlaps with urology, reproductive medicine, endocrinology, and fertility care. In many clinical settings, an andrologist may have a background in urology with additional expertise in male reproductive medicine.

An andrology-focused consultation may address:

* Erectile dysfunction

The exact specialist you need depends on the problem. A fertility specialist, urologist, reproductive medicine specialist, or another doctor may be appropriate depending on your symptoms and diagnosis.

## Andrologist vs Urologist: What Is the Difference?

A urologist treats a broad range of urinary and male reproductive conditions, while an andrologist focuses more specifically on male reproductive and sexual health.

| Andrologist | Urologist |
| --- | --- |
| Focuses on male reproductive health | Treats urinary and male reproductive conditions |
| Male infertility | Kidney and urinary conditions |
| Sperm production and quality | Prostate conditions |
| Male reproductive hormones | Urinary tract problems |
| Sexual and reproductive concerns | Kidney and bladder disorders |
| Fertility-related evaluation | Urological surgery and procedures |

There is considerable overlap between the two fields. For example, a man with infertility caused by a varicocele may need expertise that involves both reproductive medicine and urology.

The right choice should therefore depend on **why you are seeking care**, rather than the title alone.

## What Does an Andrologist Check?

The evaluation depends on your symptoms and medical history. There is no single test that answers every question about male fertility.

### Medical and Reproductive History

Your doctor may ask about:

* Previous operations
* Genital or reproductive infections

This history helps determine which tests are useful and which are unlikely to add meaningful information.

### Semen Analysis

A semen analysis examines a semen sample and provides information about sperm and semen characteristics.

It commonly assesses:

* Sperm concentration
* Total sperm number
* Sperm motility, meaning how well sperm move
* Sperm morphology, meaning sperm shape and structure
* Semen volume
* Other laboratory characteristics

WHO's sixth edition laboratory manual provides standardized guidance for semen examination and processing.

A semen analysis is important, but it is not a complete fertility diagnosis by itself. One abnormal result does not automatically mean that pregnancy is impossible. Results can vary between samples and need to be interpreted alongside the man's history and his partner's fertility assessment.

For a deeper explanation, see **[male fertility testing and what the results mean](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/male-infertility--treatment)**.

### Hormone Testing

Hormones can influence sperm production and sexual function.

Depending on the clinical picture, a doctor may consider tests involving hormones such as testosterone, FSH, or LH. These tests are not automatically required for every man.

The reason for testing matters. A hormone result needs to be interpreted alongside symptoms, examination findings, semen results, and medical history.

### Physical Examination

A physical examination may help identify findings that cannot be detected through a blood test or semen analysis.

Depending on the situation, the doctor may assess the testes and other relevant parts of the reproductive system.

### Imaging

Imaging is not required for every fertility patient.

For example, a scrotal ultrasound may be considered when there is a reason to investigate a physical problem such as a suspected varicocele or another testicular concern.

The decision should be based on clinical findings rather than a routine checklist.

## What Are the Common Causes of Male Infertility?

Male infertility can have several causes, and sometimes more than one factor is involved.

Common possibilities include:

* Low sperm concentration
* Poor sperm motility
* Abnormal sperm morphology
* Problems with sperm production
* Hormonal disorders
* Blockages in the reproductive tract
* Varicocele
* Previous infections
* Testicular injury
* Previous testicular or groin surgery
* Certain medical treatments
* Genetic factors
* Sexual or ejaculation problems
* Lifestyle and environmental factors

WHO identifies problems involving sperm production, sperm movement or shape, reproductive tract obstruction, and hormonal disorders among possible causes of male infertility.

Sometimes testing does not identify one clear cause. This does not mean that there are no possible treatment options.

## Can Erectile Dysfunction Affect Fertility?

Erectile dysfunction and infertility are different medical problems, but they can occur together.

Erectile dysfunction means difficulty getting or maintaining an erection suitable for sexual activity. It can make intercourse difficult or prevent sperm from being deposited during intercourse.

A man can also have normal erections and still have abnormal sperm parameters.

That distinction matters. **Sexual performance does not tell you whether sperm production is normal.**

If erectile difficulties occur alongside difficulty conceiving, both concerns should be mentioned during the consultation.

## Can Low Testosterone Affect Male Fertility?

Low testosterone and male fertility are related, but they are not the same thing.

Testosterone plays an important role in male reproductive function. However, sperm production is controlled by a more complex hormonal system involving the brain, pituitary gland, and testes.

This is one reason fertility treatment should not be based on a single hormone result.

If low testosterone is suspected, a doctor can determine whether hormone testing is appropriate and investigate possible causes.

## Common Myths About Andrology and Male Fertility

### Myth 1: A normal erection means a man is fertile

**Fact:** Erectile function and sperm production are different processes. A man can have normal erections while having abnormal sperm parameters.

### Myth 2: A low sperm count means you cannot have children

**Fact:** A low sperm count can affect fertility, but it does not automatically mean natural pregnancy is impossible.

### Myth 3: Fertility testing is mainly for women

**Fact:** Male factors can contribute to infertility. WHO recommends an approach that considers male and female reproductive factors.

### Myth 4: One abnormal semen analysis proves infertility

**Fact:** One semen analysis is not a complete diagnosis. Results can vary, and a doctor may recommend repeat testing or further assessment.

### Myth 5: Every man needs a complete fertility test panel

**Fact:** Additional investigations should be selected according to the medical history, examination, and initial findings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does an andrologist treat?

An andrologist focuses on male reproductive and sexual health. Concerns may include male infertility, sperm abnormalities, reproductive hormones, erectile difficulties, ejaculation problems, and other male reproductive conditions.

### When should a man see an andrologist?

A consultation may be useful when a couple has difficulty conceiving, a semen analysis is abnormal, or a man has sexual, hormonal, testicular, or reproductive concerns. Earlier assessment may be appropriate when there is a known risk factor.

### What is the main test for male fertility?

Semen analysis is one of the main initial investigations. It assesses sperm concentration, motility, morphology, semen volume, and other characteristics. It is one part of a broader fertility assessment.

### Is an andrologist the same as a urologist?

No. There is significant overlap, but urology covers a broader range of urinary and male reproductive conditions. Andrology focuses more specifically on male reproductive and sexual health.

### Can erectile dysfunction cause infertility?

Erectile dysfunction can interfere with intercourse and therefore make conception difficult. It does not necessarily mean that sperm production is abnormal, so both issues may need separate assessment.

### Can low testosterone cause fertility problems?

Hormonal problems can affect sperm production and reproductive function. However, testosterone levels should be interpreted in context, and treatment decisions should be made after proper medical assessment.

### Can low sperm count be treated?

The appropriate treatment depends on the cause. A doctor may recommend further testing, treatment of an underlying condition, or fertility treatment when appropriate.

### Can male infertility be treated without IVF?

Sometimes. Treatment depends on the cause and may include treating an underlying condition, addressing certain male fertility problems, or using fertility treatments such as IUI or IVF with ICSI when clinically appropriate.

### Is one abnormal semen analysis enough to diagnose infertility?

No. Semen results can vary between samples, and one abnormal result does not by itself establish that a man is infertile. Further evaluation or repeat testing may be appropriate.

### Should both partners be assessed for infertility?

Often, yes. Fertility can be affected by male factors, female factors, or a combination of both. Assessing both partners can provide a more complete picture.
