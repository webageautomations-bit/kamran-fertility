## Fertility Consultation in Lahore: What to Expect

Trying to conceive can become stressful when pregnancy does not happen as expected. Many couples are unsure whether they should wait longer, have fertility tests, or see a specialist.

A **[fertility consultation in Lahore](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/assessment)** helps identify possible reasons for delayed conception and decide which tests, if any, are appropriate. Fertility problems can involve the woman, the man, both partners, or sometimes remain unexplained. WHO estimates that infertility affects about **one in six people of reproductive age worldwide**.

A good consultation should therefore look at **both partners** rather than assuming the problem is related to the woman.

## Quick Answer: What Happens During a Fertility Consultation?

A fertility consultation usually starts with a detailed medical and reproductive history. Depending on the couple's age, symptoms, previous pregnancies, menstrual pattern and medical history, the doctor may recommend semen analysis, assessment of ovulation, ultrasound, hormone testing or tests of the fallopian tubes.

The purpose is not to order every available test. Current fertility guidance supports a systematic evaluation that begins with the **most useful and least invasive investigations**.

## When Should You Have a Fertility Consultation in Lahore?

You should consider a fertility consultation after **12 months** of regular, unprotected intercourse without pregnancy if the woman is under 35. Evaluation is generally recommended after **six months** when the woman is 35 or older, while women over 40 may need more immediate assessment.

You should not necessarily wait that long if there is already a known or suspected fertility problem.

Earlier assessment may be appropriate with:

* Irregular or absent periods
* Suspected ovulation problems
* Endometriosis
* Previous pelvic or tubal problems
* Previous chemotherapy or radiation
* Recurrent pregnancy loss
* Known male fertility problems
* Sexual or ejaculation difficulties
* Previous fertility treatment
* A history that may affect ovarian or sperm function

NICE also recommends specialist referral at presentation when there is a suspected or known clinical cause of infertility or other important risk factors.

## What Does a Fertility Specialist Check?

A fertility evaluation looks at several parts of reproductive health rather than one single test.

### Ovulation and Menstrual Cycles

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. Regular menstrual cycles often suggest that ovulation is occurring, but irregular periods can indicate an ovulation disorder.

The doctor may review cycle length, bleeding pattern and other symptoms. Blood tests or ultrasound may be used when clinically appropriate. NICE recommends progesterone testing during the luteal phase when investigating infertility, with timing adjusted to the person's cycle.

### Ovarian Reserve

Ovarian reserve describes the remaining supply of eggs and their expected response to fertility treatment.

Tests such as **AMH and antral follicle count** can help clinicians assess ovarian response when planning assisted conception. They should not be treated as a simple test that predicts whether someone can naturally become pregnant. NICE's 2026 guidance specifically advises against using AMH to predict spontaneous pregnancy.

Age remains a major factor in female fertility and should be considered alongside test results.

### Fallopian Tubes and Uterus

The fallopian tubes provide the pathway where sperm and egg can meet. If the tubes are blocked or damaged, natural conception may be affected.

Depending on the medical history, a doctor may recommend a test such as **hysterosalpingography (HSG)** or another method of assessing tubal patency. ASRM includes assessment of the reproductive tract and tubal patency as part of a complete infertility evaluation.

The uterus may also be assessed when there is a reason to suspect a structural problem.

## Why Should Men Be Evaluated for Infertility?

Male factors contribute to infertility in a substantial proportion of couples, so evaluating only the woman can delay the right diagnosis.

A **[male infertility specialist in Lahore](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/male-infertility--treatment)** may assess sperm production, sperm movement, semen volume and sperm shape, together with the man's medical and reproductive history.

A **semen analysis** is usually one of the first investigations for male fertility. It provides information about sperm concentration, movement and morphology. WHO-based reference ranges are used when interpreting semen results, but a single semen value does not by itself determine whether pregnancy is possible.

If an initial semen analysis is abnormal, NICE recommends a repeat confirmatory test. The timing depends on the severity of the abnormality and the clinical situation.

## What Tests May Be Included in a Fertility Consultation?

Not every patient needs every test. The investigation should be tailored to the couple.

Common assessments can include:

| Assessment | What it helps evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| Medical and reproductive history | Previous pregnancy, illnesses, surgery and fertility risks |
| Menstrual history | Cycle regularity and possible ovulation problems |
| Semen analysis | Sperm concentration, movement and morphology |

A specialist should explain **why** a test is being recommended rather than simply ordering a large panel of investigations.

## What Fertility Treatments Might Be Discussed?

Treatment depends on the cause, the couple's age, reproductive history and test results.

Possible approaches include:

* Timed intercourse when appropriate and when ovulation can be identified.
* Treatment of ovulation problems when a specific disorder is identified.
* Surgical treatment for selected structural or tubal conditions.
* **Intrauterine insemination (IUI)** in suitable circumstances.
* **In vitro fertilisation (IVF)** when IVF is clinically appropriate.
* **Intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)** in selected cases involving sperm-related factors or specific treatment circumstances.

IVF is not automatically the best first treatment. The appropriate option depends on the diagnosis and individual circumstances. ## What Age Is Best for IVF?

There is no single age at which IVF is automatically "best" for everyone.

Female age is a major factor in fertility and also affects outcomes with assisted conception. NICE recommends considering maternal age when assessing the likelihood of pregnancy and using ovarian reserve tests mainly to help predict ovarian response and guide treatment decisions.

For this reason, delaying a fertility assessment simply to wait for a particular age or treatment can sometimes reduce available options.

A good male infertility specialist in Lahore should look beyond the semen report when the results are abnormal or when the clinical history suggests a male factor.

## Common Fertility Myths vs Facts

### Myth: Infertility is mainly a woman's problem.

**Fact:** Fertility problems can involve male factors, female factors, both partners or unexplained causes. A parallel assessment of both partners is recommended when applicable.

### Myth: A normal period always means fertility is normal.

**Fact:** Regular cycles are reassuring for ovulation, but fertility also depends on factors such as age, sperm quality, fallopian tubes and reproductive anatomy.

### Myth: AMH tells you whether you can get pregnant naturally.

**Fact:** AMH can help assess ovarian response during fertility treatment, but current NICE guidance does not recommend using AMH as a predictor of spontaneous pregnancy.

### Myth: IVF is always the first treatment.

**Fact:** IVF is one fertility treatment among several. The appropriate treatment depends on the cause of infertility and the couple's individual circumstances.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should I try naturally before seeing a fertility specialist?

For women under 35, an infertility evaluation is generally considered after 12 months of regular, unprotected intercourse without pregnancy. At age 35 or older, evaluation is generally recommended after six months, with earlier assessment appropriate in some circumstances.

### Should the husband also attend the fertility consultation?

Yes, when possible. Fertility evaluation should consider both partners because male factors can contribute to difficulty conceiving. A semen analysis is commonly part of the initial male assessment.

### What is the first test for male infertility?

A semen analysis is generally an important first investigation. It assesses features such as sperm concentration, movement and morphology. If the result is abnormal, repeat testing and further assessment may be recommended.

### Can blocked fallopian tubes cause infertility?

Yes. Blocked or damaged fallopian tubes can prevent sperm and egg from meeting normally. Tubal assessment may therefore form part of an infertility evaluation when clinically appropriate.

### Does age affect fertility?

Yes. Female fertility declines with age, and age is a major factor when assessing both natural conception and assisted reproduction.

### Is IVF necessary for every couple with infertility?

No. Treatment depends on the underlying cause, age, reproductive history and results of the fertility evaluation. Some couples may be treated without IVF, while others may benefit from assisted reproductive techniques. ### Can infertility have no obvious cause?

Yes. Some couples complete an appropriate evaluation without a clear cause being identified. This is commonly described as unexplained infertility and can still have treatment options.
