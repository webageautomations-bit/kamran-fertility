## What Is IVF and When Is It Recommended?

**IVF, or in vitro fertilisation, is a treatment that allows fertilisation to take place in a laboratory before an embryo is transferred into the uterus.**

IVF may be considered in situations involving:

- Blocked or damaged fallopian tubes
- Some forms of male-factor infertility
- Certain ovulation problems
- Endometriosis
- Reduced fertility associated with age
- Some cases of unexplained infertility
- Previous unsuccessful fertility treatment
- Situations where other treatment options are unlikely to be suitable

Infertility is not exclusively a female issue. Male factors can contribute to difficulty conceiving, which is why assessment of both partners may be important.

## What Happens During IVF Treatment?

An IVF cycle involves several stages. The exact protocol can differ according to the patient's fertility history and response to treatment.

### 1. Fertility assessment and planning

The first stage is understanding the couple's medical and reproductive history.

The doctor may review previous pregnancies, fertility treatments, menstrual history, medical conditions, medications, and previous test results.

Investigations are then selected according to the patient's circumstances.

### 2. Ovarian stimulation

Medication may be prescribed to encourage the ovaries to develop multiple follicles.

The response is monitored during the cycle using clinical assessment and ultrasound. The aim is to develop suitable follicles while maintaining appropriate medical supervision.

### 3. Egg retrieval

When the follicles reach the appropriate stage, the eggs are collected through an egg-retrieval procedure.

The eggs are then taken to the embryology laboratory for fertilisation.

### 4. Fertilisation

The eggs may be fertilised using conventional IVF or, where clinically appropriate, **ICSI**.

ICSI stands for intracytoplasmic sperm injection. It involves injecting a single sperm directly into an egg and may be considered when specific sperm-related factors or previous fertilisation problems make it appropriate.

### 5. Embryo development

After fertilisation, embryos are observed as they develop in the laboratory.

The embryology team assesses their development before the next stage of treatment.

### 6. Embryo transfer

A selected embryo may then be transferred into the uterus.

Depending on the treatment plan, suitable embryos may also be frozen for possible future use.

The number of embryos transferred should be determined according to the clinical circumstances and applicable fertility guidance.

## What Is the Difference Between IVF and IUI?

**IUI and IVF are different fertility treatments, and neither is automatically the best option for every couple.**

IUI, or intrauterine insemination, involves placing prepared sperm directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation.

IVF involves collecting eggs, fertilising them in a laboratory, developing embryos, and transferring an embryo into the uterus.

The choice depends on the cause of infertility and the couple's circumstances.

For a clearer comparison, read **[IVF vs IUI: understanding the difference](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources)**.

IUI may be considered in selected cases where pregnancy through less invasive treatment remains reasonable. IVF may be more appropriate when particular fertility factors make IUI less suitable.

The decision should therefore follow assessment rather than simply choosing the treatment that sounds more advanced.

## When Is ICSI Used During IVF?

**ICSI is an additional laboratory technique used in selected IVF cases, particularly when sperm-related factors or previous fertilisation problems make it appropriate.**

During conventional IVF, sperm and eggs are placed together so fertilisation can occur.

During ICSI, an embryologist injects one sperm directly into an egg.

ICSI may be considered when semen parameters are significantly abnormal or when a previous IVF cycle resulted in failed or very low fertilisation.

It should not be presented as an automatic upgrade that every IVF patient needs.

The reason for using ICSI should be explained as part of the individual treatment plan.

## Can IVF Guarantee Pregnancy?

**No. IVF cannot guarantee pregnancy or a live birth.**

Treatment outcomes vary according to factors such as age, egg and sperm factors, ovarian reserve, embryo development, underlying fertility problems, and previous treatment.

This is why responsible fertility care focuses on individual assessment rather than promising a particular outcome.

IVF can provide an important treatment option for couples who need assisted reproduction, but every patient's situation is different.

## Common IVF Myths vs Facts

### Myth: IVF is always the first fertility treatment.

**Fact:** IVF is one of several fertility treatments. The appropriate option depends on the underlying fertility problem and previous treatment.

### Myth: Infertility is mainly a woman's problem.

**Fact:** Male factors can contribute to infertility. Both partners may need assessment.

### Myth: The cheapest IVF package is always the best option.

**Fact:** Prices can cover different services. Compare the complete treatment cost rather than the advertised starting price.

### Myth: Everyone undergoing IVF needs ICSI.

**Fact:** ICSI is a specific laboratory technique. It may be appropriate in selected circumstances but is not automatically required for every IVF cycle.

### Myth: A clinic can guarantee an IVF pregnancy.

**Fact:** No responsible fertility provider can guarantee a pregnancy. Outcomes vary between individuals.
