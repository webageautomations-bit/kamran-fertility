## Which Male Sexual Health Problems Need Medical Attention?

Persistent or troublesome sexual symptoms deserve medical assessment rather than self-treatment.

### Erectile Dysfunction

Erectile dysfunction, or ED, means having difficulty getting or keeping an erection that is sufficient for sexual activity.

Occasional difficulty can happen for many reasons. Stress, tiredness, relationship difficulties, alcohol, and other temporary factors can affect sexual function.

Repeated or persistent erectile problems may need further assessment because erectile function can be affected by blood vessels, hormones, medications, neurological conditions, psychological factors, and other health issues.

A consultation can help determine whether further evaluation is appropriate.

### Low Sexual Desire

A noticeable reduction in sexual desire can have several possible causes.

Hormonal changes, stress, relationship difficulties, certain medications, sleep problems, and other medical conditions may contribute. Testosterone testing is not automatically needed for every man with reduced libido; testing is considered according to the clinical situation.

### Ejaculation Problems

Ejaculation concerns can include premature ejaculation, delayed ejaculation, inability to ejaculate, or pain associated with ejaculation.

The underlying causes can differ considerably. A specialist may ask about when the problem started, whether it happens every time, medications, previous surgery, infections, and other symptoms.

### Genital or Urinary Symptoms

Pain, swelling, discharge, changes in the testicles, urinary difficulties, or other genital symptoms should not be ignored.

Some symptoms require prompt medical assessment, particularly if they are severe, sudden, or associated with fever or significant pain.

## What Causes Sexual Health Problems in Men?

Male sexual health problems can result from physical, hormonal, psychological, reproductive, or medication-related factors.

Possible contributors include:

* Hormonal disorders
* Diabetes and metabolic conditions
* Cardiovascular or blood-flow problems
* Certain medications
* Neurological conditions
* Genital or urinary infections
* Testicular problems
* Previous pelvic or genital surgery
* Stress and anxiety
* Relationship difficulties
* Sleep problems
* Certain lifestyle factors

The same symptom can have different causes in different men. That is why treatment should follow an assessment rather than a symptom alone.

For example, erectile dysfunction does not automatically mean low testosterone, and low sexual desire does not automatically mean a hormone problem.

## What Tests Might Be Recommended?

The appropriate tests depend on the symptoms and findings. There is no single test that evaluates every aspect of male sexual health.

### Semen Analysis

A semen analysis examines characteristics of semen and sperm, including sperm concentration, movement, and morphology.

The WHO describes semen analysis as an important laboratory investigation for assessing male fertility. Its laboratory manual provides standardized methods for semen examination.

If fertility is part of the concern, semen analysis may therefore form part of the initial male fertility assessment.

### Hormone Testing

Hormone testing may be considered when symptoms or examination findings suggest a possible hormonal cause.

Depending on the clinical situation, testosterone and other reproductive hormones may be assessed. NICE guidance recommends considering testosterone and gonadotrophin testing in men with repeated abnormal semen analyses alongside appropriate clinical assessment.

### Infection Testing

If symptoms or sexual history suggest an infection, appropriate testing may be recommended.

The type of testing depends on the symptoms and suspected infection. Not every patient needs the same infection screen.

### Physical Examination

A physical examination may be appropriate when symptoms, fertility concerns, or abnormal test results require further assessment.

For men with repeated abnormal semen analyses, current NICE guidance recommends examination of the scrotum and testes as part of the assessment.

## Can Sexual Health Problems Affect Fertility?

Yes. Some sexual health problems can make conception more difficult, but sexual dysfunction and infertility are not the same condition.

For example, erectile dysfunction may make intercourse difficult, while ejaculation problems can affect the delivery of sperm. At the same time, a man can have normal sexual function but have an abnormal semen analysis.

Male fertility assessment therefore looks beyond sexual performance.

The WHO's 2025 infertility guideline recommends a structured assessment of male factors that includes medical history, physical assessment, and semen analysis where appropriate.

### What If the Semen Analysis Is Abnormal?

One abnormal semen analysis does not automatically explain the entire fertility situation.

Semen parameters can vary, and interpretation should consider the overall clinical picture. Current WHO guidance suggests repeating semen analysis after a minimum of 11 weeks when one or more semen parameters are outside the WHO reference ranges.

NICE similarly recommends a repeat confirmatory test after an abnormal initial result, ideally around three months later in many cases, while noting that severe abnormalities may require earlier repeat testing.

The purpose of repeat testing is not simply to produce another number. It helps determine whether an abnormal result is persistent and whether further evaluation is needed.

## When Should You See a Male Sexual Health Specialist in Lahore?

You do not need to wait until a problem becomes severe before asking for medical advice.

Consider arranging a consultation if you have:

* Persistent difficulty getting or maintaining an erection
* A significant or ongoing change in sexual desire
* Recurrent ejaculation problems
* Pain with ejaculation
* Genital pain, swelling, or other unusual symptoms
* Concerns about sexually transmitted infections
* Abnormal semen analysis results
* Difficulty conceiving with your partner
* Previous testicular injury or surgery
* Concerns about sperm health
* Questions about sexual or reproductive health that you have been unable to discuss elsewhere

The timing of fertility assessment also matters. WHO defines infertility as failure to achieve pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse.

However, couples do not always need to wait 12 months before seeking advice. Previous reproductive problems, known medical conditions, abnormal semen results, or other concerns may justify earlier assessment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does a male sexual health specialist do?

A male sexual health specialist assesses concerns involving erections, libido, ejaculation, genital health, and male reproductive health. The evaluation may also identify medical or hormonal factors contributing to symptoms.

### Who is the best male sexologist in Lahore?

There is no single objective measure that determines the "best" doctor for every patient. Look for relevant qualifications, experience, confidentiality, evidence-based assessment, and clear communication about tests and treatment options.

### Should I see a sexologist or fertility specialist for sperm problems?

If your main concern is sperm count, sperm movement, semen results, or difficulty conceiving, a fertility specialist with experience in male reproductive health can assess the fertility side of the problem. The appropriate specialist can also advise whether another referral is needed.

### Can erectile dysfunction cause infertility?

Erectile dysfunction does not necessarily cause infertility. However, if an erection is not sufficient for intercourse, it can make natural conception more difficult. Erectile dysfunction and abnormal sperm production can also occur at the same time but are separate issues.

### What is checked during a male fertility assessment?

Depending on the individual situation, assessment may include medical and reproductive history, semen analysis, physical examination, hormone testing, and other investigations when clinically indicated. WHO recommends a structured approach to evaluating male factors in couples with infertility.

### Does every man with sexual problems need hormone tests?

No. Hormone testing depends on the symptoms, examination findings, fertility history, and other clinical factors. A doctor can determine whether testing is appropriate rather than ordering a routine hormone panel for everyone.

### Is a male sexual health consultation confidential?

Sexual and reproductive health concerns should be handled respectfully and confidentially. At Kamran Fertility Clinic, confidential assessment is part of the clinic's approach to men's reproductive health.

### When should I seek help for male fertility?

If you have been trying to conceive for 12 months without pregnancy, an infertility assessment is generally appropriate. Earlier medical advice may be reasonable when there are known fertility problems, abnormal semen results, previous testicular problems, or other relevant concerns.
