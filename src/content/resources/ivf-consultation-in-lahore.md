## What Happens During an IVF Consultation in Lahore?

An IVF consultation usually begins with a detailed discussion about your fertility history and previous treatment. The doctor then decides which examinations or investigations are needed before choosing a treatment plan.

The consultation may cover:

* How long you have been trying to conceive
* Previous pregnancies, miscarriages, or fertility treatments
* Menstrual cycle pattern
* Previous pelvic infections or surgeries
* Medical conditions and medications
* Previous ultrasound or hormone results
* Sperm testing and male fertility history
* Previous IUI or IVF cycles
* Family history relevant to fertility
* Your age and reproductive goals

## Do You Need IVF Immediately After the Consultation?

Not necessarily. The purpose of an IVF consultation is to determine whether IVF is appropriate, not to assume that every patient needs IVF.

Depending on the cause of infertility, possible approaches can include:

* Treating an underlying ovulation problem
* Addressing a male fertility factor
* Treating certain tubal or uterine problems
* Considering [intrauterine insemination (IUI)](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/ivf-vs-iui)
* Proceeding to IVF when appropriate
* Considering other assisted reproductive options in selected cases

The right treatment depends on factors such as age, fertility history, ovarian function, sperm results, tubal status, and previous treatment. NICE and ASRM both emphasize systematic fertility assessment before selecting treatment.

## Which Tests May Be Needed Before IVF?

The tests needed before IVF vary from patient to patient. A doctor may request tests that assess ovulation, ovarian reserve, the reproductive tract, and sperm.

### 1. Ovarian Reserve Testing

Ovarian reserve describes the remaining egg supply available in the ovaries. Tests may include AMH, antral follicle count on ultrasound, and sometimes FSH and estradiol.

These tests can help guide fertility treatment, but they do not provide a simple prediction of whether someone will become pregnant naturally. ASRM notes that ovarian reserve tests can help guide treatment but are poor predictors of natural fertility.

### 2. Pelvic Ultrasound

A transvaginal ultrasound can provide information about the uterus and ovaries. It may also assess the antral follicle count, which refers to the small follicles visible in the ovaries.

The findings can help the fertility specialist plan further investigation or treatment.

### 3. Tubal Assessment

Some patients need testing to determine whether the fallopian tubes are open. Hysterosalpingography (HSG) is one test used to assess tubal patency.

Not every person preparing for IVF needs the same tubal testing. The decision depends on the clinical situation and whether the result would change management. ASRM includes assessment of tubal patency as part of the fertility evaluation when indicated.

### 4. Semen Analysis

A [semen analysis](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/male-infertility--treatment) evaluates sperm-related factors, including sperm count and movement. It is an important part of fertility assessment because male factors can contribute to difficulty conceiving.

If there is a known or suspected male fertility issue, evaluation should happen alongside the female partner's assessment rather than being delayed.

## When Should You Seek a Fertility Consultation?

The appropriate time depends on age, medical history, and individual circumstances.

For women under 35, infertility evaluation is generally considered after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse without pregnancy. For women aged 35 or older, evaluation is generally recommended after six months. Women over 40 may benefit from more immediate evaluation.

Earlier assessment can also be appropriate when there is a known or suspected fertility problem, such as irregular or absent periods, suspected endometriosis, previous pelvic disease, or known male subfertility.

**You do not necessarily need to wait 12 months if there is already a reason to suspect a fertility problem.**

## What Is the IVF Treatment Process?

IVF means in vitro fertilization, where eggs and sperm are brought together in a laboratory to create embryos. An embryo may then be transferred into the uterus as part of the treatment.

The exact protocol differs between patients, but an IVF cycle commonly involves:

1. **Ovarian stimulation** – medicines are used to encourage development of multiple follicles.
2. **Monitoring** – ultrasound and, when appropriate, blood tests are used to monitor ovarian response.
3. **Egg retrieval** – mature eggs are collected from the ovaries.
4. **Fertilization** – eggs are combined with sperm in the laboratory.
5. **Embryo development** – embryos are monitored as they develop.
6. **Embryo transfer** – an embryo may be transferred into the uterus.
7. **Pregnancy testing and follow-up** – follow-up is arranged according to the treatment plan.

IVF has established benefits for selected fertility problems, but it also has risks and does not guarantee pregnancy. Monitoring during ovarian stimulation is used to assess the response and reduce treatment-related risks.

## What Affects IVF Treatment Decisions?

IVF treatment is not based on one test or one number. Several factors may influence the recommended approach.

These can include:

| Factor | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Age | Female age is an important factor in reproductive potential |
| Ovarian reserve | Helps guide ovarian stimulation and treatment planning |
| Sperm results | May affect fertilization strategy |
| Tubal condition | Helps identify whether tubal disease is contributing |
| Uterine findings | May affect embryo transfer planning |
| Previous fertility treatment | Previous response can inform future treatment |
| Medical history | Certain conditions may change treatment decisions |
| Previous IVF results | Prior response and embryo development may guide another cycle |

This is why a fertility specialist should review the complete clinical picture rather than recommending treatment from one laboratory result.

## Should Both Partners Attend the Consultation?

When possible, both partners should be involved in fertility assessment.

Infertility can involve female factors, male factors, both partners, or no clearly identifiable cause. A semen analysis is therefore an important part of many fertility evaluations rather than something that should only be considered after female testing is complete.

Approaching fertility as a couple can also make treatment decisions clearer because both partners understand the findings and available options.

## IVF Consultation vs Fertility Assessment: What Is the Difference?

A [fertility assessment](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/assessment) focuses on identifying possible reasons for difficulty conceiving. An IVF consultation may include that assessment but also focuses on whether IVF or another assisted reproductive treatment is appropriate.

| Fertility assessment | IVF consultation |
| --- | --- |
| Investigates possible causes | Reviews whether IVF is appropriate |
| May involve both partners | Considers both partners where relevant |
| Reviews medical and reproductive history | Reviews previous fertility treatment |
| May involve hormone tests and ultrasound | May include discussion of IVF protocols |
| Can lead to several treatment options | Focuses more closely on assisted reproduction |

Patients should not assume that an IVF consultation means they have already decided to undergo IVF.

## Common Myths About IVF Consultations

### Myth 1: Everyone with infertility needs IVF

**Fact:** IVF is one treatment option. Depending on the cause and individual circumstances, other approaches may be appropriate.

### Myth 2: A low AMH means pregnancy is impossible

**Fact:** AMH helps assess ovarian reserve and can guide treatment, but it should not be treated as a simple yes-or-no fertility test.

### Myth 3: Fertility problems are mainly a woman's issue

**Fact:** Male factors can contribute to infertility, which is why semen evaluation is commonly part of fertility assessment.

### Myth 4: IVF guarantees a baby

**Fact:** IVF can help people with several types of fertility problems, but no responsible fertility specialist can guarantee a pregnancy or live birth.
