## What Does an IVF Doctor Actually Do?

An IVF doctor assesses the causes of infertility, decides whether IVF is suitable, plans treatment, monitors the ovarian response, and guides the couple through egg retrieval and embryo transfer when IVF is performed.

IVF, or in vitro fertilization, is a form of assisted reproductive treatment. Eggs are collected from the ovaries and fertilized with sperm in a laboratory. An embryo is then transferred into the uterus.

However, IVF is not simply a procedure performed on the woman's eggs.

A proper fertility assessment may consider:

* The woman's age and menstrual history
* Ovulation and ovarian function
* Ovarian reserve, meaning an estimate of the remaining egg supply
* The condition of the uterus
* Whether the fallopian tubes are open
* Previous pregnancies or fertility treatments
* The man's semen analysis
* Sperm count, movement, and shape
* Previous surgeries, infections, or reproductive conditions
* Medical conditions that may affect pregnancy

WHO reports that infertility can arise from male factors, female factors, combined factors, or causes that remain unexplained.

That is why a doctor should not recommend IVF simply because a couple has struggled to conceive.

## When Is IVF Recommended?

IVF may be recommended when other fertility treatments are unlikely to work, have failed, or when the underlying fertility problem makes IVF a reasonable option.

Common situations can include:

* Blocked or severely damaged fallopian tubes
* Some cases of severe male-factor infertility
* Certain cases of endometriosis
* Some ovulation disorders when other treatment has not worked
* Unexplained infertility after appropriate evaluation
* Previous unsuccessful fertility treatment
* Situations where IVF offers a reasonable treatment option based on age and fertility factors

The decision depends on the individual couple.

NICE guidance emphasizes that the chance of IVF success is influenced by factors including age and previous treatment history.

A fertility consultation should therefore answer a more useful question than "Can I do IVF?"

The better question is: **"Is IVF the most appropriate treatment for us, and why?"**

## What Happens During an IVF Cycle?

A standard IVF cycle involves several stages, although the exact protocol differs between patients.

### Step 1: Fertility assessment

The doctor reviews the couple's history and relevant test results.

This stage helps determine whether IVF is suitable and what treatment approach may be needed.

### Step 2: Ovarian stimulation

Medicines are used to stimulate the ovaries so that several follicles can develop.

A follicle is a small fluid-filled structure in the ovary that can contain an egg.

The doctor monitors the response using ultrasound and, when appropriate, blood tests.

### Step 3: Egg retrieval

When the follicles have developed appropriately, the eggs are collected using a procedure guided by ultrasound.

The retrieved eggs are then taken to the IVF laboratory.

### Step 4: Fertilization

The eggs are combined with sperm in the laboratory.

In selected cases, ICSI may be used. The choice depends on the couple's fertility findings and the laboratory team's assessment.

### Step 5: Embryo development

A fertilized egg can develop into an embryo.

Some embryos may continue developing to the blastocyst stage, which is a later stage of embryo development.

Not every retrieved egg becomes a usable embryo. This is one reason doctors should avoid promising that a particular number of embryos will result from treatment.

### Step 6: Embryo transfer

A suitable embryo may be transferred into the uterus.

In other situations, embryos may be frozen for later use. A frozen embryo transfer means an embryo that was previously frozen is transferred during a later treatment cycle.

The exact approach depends on the patient's medical situation and the treatment plan.

## Is IVF 100% Successful?

**No. IVF is not 100% successful.**

The chance of achieving a pregnancy or live birth varies between individuals and treatment cycles. Age is one important factor, particularly because egg quantity and egg quality change with increasing age.

Other factors can include:

* Ovarian reserve
* Sperm quality
* Embryo development
* Uterine factors
* Previous fertility treatment
* Underlying medical conditions
* The number and quality of embryos available

NICE states that IVF success is partly dependent on age and that success can decline with unsuccessful previous cycles.

This is why a clinic should be careful with statements such as "guaranteed pregnancy" or a single success percentage presented without context.

A meaningful discussion should explain which outcome is being measured, for which age group, and whether the figure refers to pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, or live birth.

## What Risks Should Couples Discuss Before IVF?

IVF is an established medical treatment, but it is not risk-free.

One recognized complication is ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS). This occurs when the ovaries respond too strongly to stimulation medicines. Severe cases can cause significant fluid shifts and require medical treatment.

Doctors can assess individual risk and adjust treatment strategies when appropriate.

Other considerations include:

* Side effects from fertility medicines
* Bleeding or infection related to procedures
* Multiple pregnancy when more than one embryo is transferred
* Pregnancy complications
* Emotional and financial stress
* The possibility that an IVF cycle does not produce a transferable embryo

ASRM describes IVF as a well-established treatment while also emphasizing that appropriate monitoring is important for managing treatment-related risks.

Your doctor should explain the relevant risks before treatment rather than after a complication occurs.

## When Should You Seek a Fertility Specialist?

You should consider a fertility consultation when pregnancy has not occurred after a period of regular unprotected intercourse, or earlier when there is a known fertility concern.

* Irregular or absent periods

WHO defines infertility as failure to achieve pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse.

Age also matters. Fertility changes over time, so couples should not assume that waiting several additional years will have no effect on treatment options.
