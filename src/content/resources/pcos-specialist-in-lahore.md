## What Is Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)?

Polycystic ovary syndrome is a hormonal condition that can affect ovulation, periods, androgen levels and metabolic health. Androgens are hormones that everyone produces, although levels and effects differ between individuals.

PCOS can cause irregular menstrual cycles, acne, increased facial or body hair, thinning scalp hair and difficulty becoming pregnant. Some people also have insulin resistance, meaning their bodies do not respond to insulin as effectively as they should.

Despite its name, PCOS does not always involve visible ovarian cysts. Ultrasound may show many small follicles, which are fluid-filled structures that contain developing eggs. Their presence alone does not confirm PCOS.

PCOS is also known as polycystic ovary syndrome or, historically, Stein–Leventhal syndrome. Some people use the term PCOD, but clinicians generally use PCOS when discussing this hormonal condition.

## What Symptoms Should Prompt You to See a PCOS Specialist?

Persistent changes in menstrual cycles, excess facial hair, acne or difficulty conceiving are reasons to seek medical advice. These symptoms can have several causes, so a consultation is more useful than assuming PCOS is responsible.

Common signs include:

* **Irregular periods:** Periods may come infrequently, vary considerably or stop for several months.
* **Excess facial or body hair:** Coarse hair may develop on the chin, upper lip, chest or abdomen.
* **Acne:** Persistent acne may affect the face, chest or back.
* **Scalp hair thinning:** Hair loss may occur in a pattern linked to higher androgen activity.
* **Difficulty becoming pregnant:** Irregular ovulation can make conception less predictable.
* **Weight changes:** Some people gain weight or find weight management difficult, although PCOS also affects people who are not overweight.
* **Darkened skin patches:** Velvety, darker skin around the neck or underarms can sometimes indicate insulin resistance.

You do not need to have every symptom. Some women have regular-looking cycles but other signs of androgen excess, while others mainly experience irregular periods.

Seek prompt medical assessment if you develop rapidly increasing facial hair, a suddenly deepening voice or other rapid changes in androgen-related features. These symptoms are not typical of ordinary PCOS and may need investigation for another cause.

## How Is PCOS Diagnosed?

Doctors diagnose PCOS by combining clinical findings, medical history and appropriate tests. No single blood test or ultrasound result confirms every case.

In adults, clinicians commonly use recognised diagnostic criteria that require at least two of the following three features, after excluding other possible causes:

* **Irregular or absent ovulation:** Periods may be infrequent or unpredictable because an egg is not released regularly.
* **Clinical or biochemical androgen excess:** Symptoms such as excess facial hair, or blood tests showing higher androgen levels.
* **Polycystic ovarian appearance on ultrasound:** The scan may show an increased number of small follicles or other recognised features.

An ultrasound is not always necessary. In adults who already have irregular ovulation and clear signs of androgen excess, a clinician may be able to diagnose PCOS without imaging.

Diagnosis differs in adolescents. Acne and irregular periods can be normal during puberty, so clinicians use more cautious criteria and avoid relying on ovarian ultrasound alone.

Your doctor may also investigate thyroid disease, raised prolactin or other conditions that can resemble PCOS. The exact tests depend on your symptoms and medical history.

## Which PCOS Tests Might You Need in Lahore?

The right PCOS tests depend on your symptoms and what the doctor needs to rule out. A test is useful when its result can clarify the diagnosis, identify a health risk or change the treatment plan.

| Test or assessment | Why a doctor may recommend it |
| --- | --- |
| Medical history and menstrual review | To understand cycle patterns, symptoms and reproductive goals |
| Physical examination | To assess relevant signs of androgen excess or other health concerns |
| Total or free testosterone | To investigate higher androgen levels |
| Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) | To check for thyroid problems that can affect periods |
| Prolactin | To investigate certain causes of irregular or absent periods |
| Blood glucose testing or HbA1c | To assess blood sugar and diabetes risk |
| Lipid profile | To assess cholesterol and other blood fats |
| Pelvic ultrasound | To examine the ovaries and pelvis when indicated |
| Additional hormone tests | To investigate other possible causes when symptoms suggest them |

Not everyone needs every test listed here. Your doctor may choose different tests depending on your age, symptoms, medication use and whether you are trying to conceive. If you are unsure which investigations are relevant, a **[fertility assessment and diagnosis](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/assessment)** can help narrow them down.

### What should you know before a PCOS test?

Ask the laboratory or clinic whether any preparation is required. Some tests depend on the time of day or menstrual cycle, while others can be performed without special preparation.

Tell your doctor about medicines, supplements, hormonal contraception and recent menstrual changes. These details can affect how certain results are interpreted.

A normal ultrasound does not automatically rule out PCOS, and an ultrasound showing small follicles does not prove that you have it.

## What PCOS Treatments Are Available in Pakistan?

PCOS treatment depends on your main symptoms, health risks and whether you want to become pregnant. There is no single treatment that suits every patient, and management often changes as your priorities change.

### Treatment for irregular periods

Hormonal contraception or prescribed progestogen treatment may help regulate bleeding and protect the lining of the uterus in selected patients. The choice depends on your medical history, pregnancy plans and individual risk factors.

Long gaps between periods deserve attention. When ovulation is infrequent, the uterine lining may remain exposed to hormonal stimulation for too long, increasing the risk of endometrial hyperplasia, a thickening of the lining that can sometimes become serious.

### Treatment for acne and unwanted hair

Doctors may recommend hormonal contraception or other medicines for selected patients with androgen-related symptoms. Improvement usually takes time because hair growth and skin changes do not respond immediately.

Some medicines used for excess hair can harm a developing fetus. Anyone who could become pregnant should discuss contraception and pregnancy plans before starting such treatment.

### Treatment for insulin resistance and metabolic risks

Lifestyle support, blood sugar monitoring and selected medicines may help manage metabolic concerns. Metformin is sometimes prescribed, particularly when metabolic factors or other clinical indications support its use.

Metformin is not necessary for every person with PCOS. Your doctor should explain what it is intended to treat and how its potential benefits apply to your situation.

### Lifestyle changes that support PCOS management

Healthy habits can support metabolic health and menstrual function, even when weight loss is not a suitable or necessary goal.

Helpful steps may include:

* Eating regular, balanced meals with vegetables, fibre and protein.
* Choosing physical activities you can maintain consistently.
* Getting enough sleep and keeping a regular sleep schedule.
* Avoiding smoking and limiting other habits that harm long-term health.
* Seeking support for stress, low mood or distress about body changes.

There is no single PCOS diet that works for everyone. Extreme dieting and unproven supplements can create additional problems, so discuss major dietary changes with a qualified healthcare professional.

## Can PCOS Affect Pregnancy and Fertility?

PCOS can make pregnancy more difficult when ovulation occurs irregularly, but it does not mean that pregnancy is impossible. Many people with PCOS conceive naturally, while others benefit from medical support. You can read more about **[PCOS and fertility](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/pcos-and-fertility)** before your consultation.

If you are trying to conceive, a fertility specialist will consider several factors rather than attributing every delay to PCOS. These may include your age, how often you ovulate, how long you have been trying, tubal factors and your partner's sperm health.

### What fertility treatments may help?

Treatment depends on the cause of infertility and your overall health. You can explore the full range of **[fertility treatment options in Lahore](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/fertility-treatment)** to see how different treatments fit different situations.

* **Ovulation induction:** Medicines help the ovaries release an egg. Letrozole is commonly recommended as a first-line option for many women with PCOS-related anovulatory infertility when no other infertility factor is present.
* **Clomiphene citrate:** This medicine can stimulate ovulation in selected patients.
* **Metformin:** It may be useful for some patients, especially when metabolic concerns are present, but it is not a universal fertility treatment.
* **[In vitro fertilisation (IVF)](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/ivf-vs-iui):** Eggs are collected and fertilised in a laboratory. IVF may be considered when other treatments are unsuitable or unsuccessful, or when additional fertility factors are present.

These treatments require a clinician's assessment and monitoring. The right approach depends on your medical history, local availability and the risks associated with each option.

A diagnosis of PCOS alone does not mean you need IVF. Your doctor should explain the simpler options that may be suitable before discussing more involved treatments.

## PCOS Myths and Facts

Several common beliefs about PCOS can cause unnecessary worry or delay appropriate care.

| Myth | Fact |
| --- | --- |
| PCOS always causes ovarian cysts. | PCOS can be diagnosed without ultrasound evidence of polycystic ovaries when other diagnostic criteria are met. |
| Only people who are overweight develop PCOS. | PCOS can affect people at different body weights. |
| PCOS always causes infertility. | PCOS can affect ovulation, but pregnancy remains possible for many people. |
| Irregular periods are harmless if they are painless. | Long gaps between periods may need assessment because they can affect the uterine lining. |
| Losing weight cures PCOS. | Weight management may help some patients, but PCOS can persist and needs care based on individual symptoms. |
| Everyone with PCOS needs the same medicine. | Treatment depends on symptoms, metabolic risks, medical history and pregnancy plans. |

## When Should You Book a Consultation?

Arrange a medical appointment if your periods repeatedly become irregular, stop for several months, or occur alongside persistent acne or excess facial hair. Seek an assessment sooner if symptoms develop rapidly or bleeding is unusually heavy.

If you are trying to conceive, discuss your reproductive plans with a qualified clinician rather than waiting for every symptom to resolve. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is the best doctor to see for PCOS?

A gynaecologist experienced in PCOS is usually the best starting point for irregular periods, acne, excess hair and reproductive concerns. An endocrinologist may be helpful when complex hormonal or metabolic problems require further assessment.

### Is Sara Ali Khan suffering from PCOS?

Public figures' health conditions should not be assumed from appearance or online discussion. Unless Sara Ali Khan has personally confirmed a diagnosis through a reliable public statement, it would be inappropriate to claim that she has PCOS.

### Can I marry a girl who has PCOS?

Yes. PCOS is a medical condition, not a reason to reject someone as a marriage partner. It can affect menstrual health and sometimes fertility, but its effects vary, and many women with PCOS have healthy relationships and pregnancies.

### Is PCOS a lifetime problem?

PCOS is generally a long-term condition, but its symptoms can change over time. Treatment can help manage menstrual problems, skin symptoms, metabolic risks and fertility concerns, although there is no guaranteed permanent cure.

### Can PCOS be diagnosed with an ultrasound alone?

No. Ultrasound findings alone are not enough to diagnose PCOS. A clinician considers symptoms, ovulation patterns, hormone findings and alternative causes before reaching a diagnosis.

### Can I get pregnant naturally with PCOS?

Yes, many women with PCOS conceive naturally. Others may need help with ovulation or additional fertility treatment, depending on their individual circumstances. Read more about **[PCOS and fertility](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/pcos-and-fertility)**.

### Which blood tests are needed for PCOS?

There is no single blood test that confirms PCOS in every patient. A doctor may request androgen testing, thyroid or prolactin tests, and blood sugar or cholesterol checks based on your symptoms and health risks.

### Does PCOS always cause weight gain?

No. PCOS can occur at any body weight. Some people experience weight gain or insulin resistance, while others do not.

### Is PCOS the same as PCOD?

The terms are often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, but PCOS is the standard clinical term for polycystic ovary syndrome. Ask your doctor what diagnostic criteria apply to your symptoms rather than relying on the label alone.

### Can PCOS increase the risk of diabetes?

PCOS is associated with a higher risk of impaired blood sugar control and type 2 diabetes in some patients. A clinician can assess your individual risk and advise whether glucose testing and follow-up are appropriate.
