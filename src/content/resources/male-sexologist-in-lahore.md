## What does a male sexologist treat?

A male sexologist deals with sexual and reproductive problems affecting men. The exact scope varies by the doctor's training, so patients should check the doctor's qualifications and area of practice.

Common concerns include:

* Erectile dysfunction
* Premature ejaculation
* Difficulty ejaculating
* Pain during ejaculation
* Reduced sexual desire
* Problems maintaining an erection
* Penile curvature or pain
* Sexual difficulties associated with anxiety or relationship stress
* Male infertility
* Low sperm count
* Problems with sperm movement or shape
* Concerns about testosterone or other reproductive hormones

Sexual symptoms can have several possible causes. For example, recurring erectile dysfunction can be associated with conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, hormone problems, medication effects, anxiety, or depression.

That is why simply treating the visible symptom may not always address the underlying problem.

## What is the difference between a sexologist and a urologist?

A sexologist focuses on sexual health, while a urologist is a medical specialist who diagnoses and treats conditions involving the urinary system and male reproductive organs.

There can be overlap between the two fields.

For example, a man with erectile dysfunction may need assessment of sexual function, blood pressure, hormones, medications, and other health factors. A man with infertility may also need assessment of the testicles, sperm production, reproductive tract, or possible obstruction.

The appropriate specialist depends on the problem. In some cases, coordinated care between specialties may be useful.

## Which sexual problems should men discuss with a doctor?

### Erectile dysfunction

Erectile dysfunction means being unable to get or maintain an erection that is adequate for sexual intercourse. Occasional erection difficulties can happen with stress or tiredness, but repeated problems deserve medical assessment.

A doctor may ask about:

* How long the problem has been present
* Whether erections occur during sleep or on waking
* Sexual desire
* Current medicines
* Smoking and alcohol use
* Diabetes or blood pressure problems
* Stress, anxiety, or relationship concerns
* Previous injuries or surgery

These questions help distinguish possible physical, hormonal, medication-related, and psychological factors.

### Premature ejaculation

Premature ejaculation involves ejaculating sooner than a man or couple would like, often with difficulty controlling ejaculation and associated distress.

It can occur for different reasons. A proper consultation helps determine whether there are contributing sexual, psychological, relationship, or medical factors.

Treatment should be based on the individual's situation rather than a generic internet remedy.

### Low sexual desire

A reduced interest in sex can have many possible causes. Stress, relationship difficulties, certain medicines, hormonal problems, depression, and other health conditions may contribute.

Low desire does not automatically mean that testosterone is low. Testing and medical history can help determine whether hormone evaluation is appropriate.

### Painful ejaculation or penile pain

Pain during ejaculation, persistent penile pain, or a new change in penile shape should not simply be ignored.

Depending on the symptoms, a doctor may consider problems involving the reproductive tract, prostate, urinary system, nerves, blood vessels, or other structures.

### Difficulty ejaculating

Some men can achieve an erection but cannot ejaculate normally. Others may notice very little semen or have changes after certain surgeries or medical treatments.

One possible cause is retrograde ejaculation, where semen travels backward into the bladder rather than leaving through the penis. Testing may sometimes identify sperm in urine after ejaculation.

## When should a man see a sexologist for fertility problems?

Male fertility should be considered when a couple has difficulty achieving pregnancy after regular unprotected intercourse.

Infertility can result from male factors, female factors, a combination of both, or causes that remain unexplained. WHO defines infertility as failure to achieve pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular unprotected sexual intercourse.

A man may have no obvious sexual symptoms and still have a fertility problem. Conversely, erectile or ejaculation problems can make conception difficult even when sperm production is normal.

This is why fertility assessment should look beyond sexual performance alone.

## What tests might a male sexologist recommend?

Testing depends on the symptoms and clinical history. There is no single test that explains every male sexual or fertility problem.

### Semen analysis

A **[semen analysis](https://www.kamranfertility.com/resources/male-infertility--treatment)** examines a semen sample for factors such as sperm count, movement, and shape. Motility means how well sperm move, while morphology refers to sperm shape.

Sperm results can vary between samples, so a doctor may recommend repeat testing when appropriate.

### Hormone testing

Blood tests may be used when symptoms or examination findings suggest a hormonal problem.

Depending on the situation, testing may include testosterone and other reproductive hormones. Hormone testing should be interpreted alongside symptoms and clinical findings rather than from one number alone.

### Physical examination

A physical examination can help identify signs that may contribute to sexual or fertility problems.

Depending on the complaint, the doctor may examine the genital area and look for findings such as testicular abnormalities or other physical causes.

### Ultrasound or other investigations

A scrotal ultrasound may be used when a doctor needs more information about the testicles or surrounding structures. It can help identify problems such as a varicocele.

Other tests, including genetic testing or specialised investigations, may be considered in selected cases, particularly when sperm counts are very low or other findings raise concern.

## How is male sexual dysfunction treated?

Treatment depends on the cause.

For erectile dysfunction, treatment may include addressing an underlying health problem, reviewing medicines, making appropriate lifestyle changes, counselling when psychological factors contribute, or using prescribed medication when suitable.

For fertility problems, treatment may involve medication, surgery, treatment of an underlying condition, sperm-retrieval procedures, or **[assisted reproductive techniques](https://www.kamranfertility.com/services/fertility-treatment)** in selected cases.

The important point is that the same treatment does not suit every man.

For example, testosterone treatment should not be started simply because a man feels tired or has reduced sexual desire. Testosterone therapy can reduce sperm production in some men, so fertility goals need to be considered before treatment.

## FAQs

### Who is the best male sexologist in Lahore?

There is no single objective way to name one doctor as the "best." A more useful approach is to compare relevant medical qualifications, experience with your specific concern, diagnostic approach, and whether the doctor provides appropriate follow-up or referral.

### What does a male sexologist treat?

A male sexologist may assess concerns such as erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation, low sexual desire, ejaculation problems, penile pain, and male fertility issues. The doctor's exact scope depends on their medical training and practice.

### Should I see a sexologist or urologist for erectile dysfunction?

Either may be appropriate depending on the cause and the doctor's area of practice. Erectile dysfunction can involve blood vessels, hormones, medications, psychological factors, or other medical conditions, so a proper assessment is more important than choosing a title alone.

### Can erectile dysfunction be treated?

In many cases, erectile dysfunction can be improved when its contributing cause is identified and treated. Options may include addressing underlying health conditions, reviewing medicines, counselling, lifestyle changes, or prescribed treatment.

### Can a sexologist diagnose male infertility?

A doctor with appropriate training in male sexual and reproductive health may assess male fertility and arrange relevant investigations. Semen analysis is a common starting test, with additional testing selected according to the findings.

### What is checked in a semen analysis?

A semen analysis can assess sperm count, movement, and morphology, along with other characteristics of the semen sample. Results can vary between samples, so repeat testing may sometimes be recommended.

### Does low sperm count mean I cannot have children?

Not necessarily. Male fertility depends on sperm production, sperm movement, sperm transport, ejaculation, and the fertility of both partners. ### Can low testosterone cause sexual problems?

Low testosterone can contribute to symptoms such as reduced sexual desire, but sexual symptoms have many possible causes. A doctor may recommend hormone testing when the history and symptoms make it appropriate.
